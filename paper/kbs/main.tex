\begin{abstract}
Class expansion must integrate few-shot novel evidence while preserving base-class accuracy. We couple vocabulary-invariant reference alignment with finite-sample admission certification. Fixed references preserve existing rankings; query normalization induces an adaptive admission margin. Preserved base rankings make protection equivalent to the retention requirement, while a direct test credits replacement readouts' recoveries. On ImageNet-R, independently tuned query normalization adds 3.60 and 5.29 percentage points of novel capacity over uncentered fusion at 95\% retention with L/14 and B/32. In the original frozen L/14 readouts, retained and dense capacities are comparable, yet directly certified policies differ by 4.85 points. Separately, original source-selected models reach 86.82\% and 58.96\% novel accuracy on ImageNet-Sketch with target calibration and empirical retention attainment in all ten partitions per encoder.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An expanding recognition system integrates unequal evidence: a trained classifier encodes accumulated base supervision, whereas arriving classes provide names and a few examples. Deployment requires novel admission without exceeding a specified loss of base recognition. Evidence must remain comparable as the vocabulary grows, and finite calibration data must support the resulting retention requirement.

Vocabulary growth can disturb evidence alignment even with frozen models. Standardizing a branch against active candidates changes its scale when a class arrives; after fusion, existing classes can exchange rank. Fixed base reference banks remove this dependence. Their query-specific statistics adapt learned, semantic and prototype evidence, while a scalar offset controls novel admission.

We develop \emph{risk-certified class expansion through reference-anchored evidence alignment}. Preserving the original base ranking makes every lost correct base prediction go to a novel class and prevents recovery of base errors. This structure makes a sufficient protection criterion exactly match the retention requirement. A second, direct accuracy test credits replacement readouts' recoveries under the same requirement. The framework exposes how recognition capacity becomes useful certified admission.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A vocabulary-invariant alignment of asymmetric evidence, with an explicit query-adaptive admission margin and an exact retention envelope for fixed readouts.
\item Finite-sample admission control for class-macro accuracy over prespecified arrivals, including a tail-coverage condition for the protection certificate and a direct test that credits recovered reference errors.
\item A capacity-to-policy analysis connecting error structure, attainable recognition and selected admission. Controlled fusion comparisons establish the alignment mechanism; source-frozen ImageNet-Sketch evaluation demonstrates target calibration without model retraining.
\end{itemize}

On ImageNet-R L/14, retained and dense readouts have comparable capacity at the minimum retention target, while their directly certified policies differ by 4.85 points. The analysis therefore concerns both recognition and its use under an accuracy requirement.

\section{Related work}
Long-tailed recognition addresses unequal class supervision through balanced fitting and losses~\cite{liu2019large,cao2019ldam,cui2019classbalanced}. CLIP supplies semantic image--text evidence~\cite{radford2021clip}, while prompt adaptation~\cite{zhou2022coop,shi2024lift}, prototypes~\cite{snell2017prototypical,wang2019simpleshot} and semantic caches~\cite{zhang2022tipadapter} adapt pre-trained representations to labeled data. Our setting combines these evidence types according to their availability at class arrival.

Class-incremental and few-shot incremental learning study retention across successive tasks~\cite{rebuffi2017icarl,tao2020fewshot}. SimpleCIL~\cite{zhou2024simplecil} and RanPAC~\cite{mcdonnell2023ranpac} show the value of pre-trained prototypes and analytic readouts. KBS approaches develop multi-feature similarity supplementation~\cite{xu2023mfs3}, sharpness-aware prototype calibration~\cite{chen2024sagg}, adaptive exploration~\cite{han2025adaptive}, representation-enhanced analytic learning~\cite{he2026real}, and multimodal prompt fusion~\cite{fang2026bpcclip}.

Recent work aligns task-specific expert inference in MoTE~\cite{li2025mote}, separates expanding feature spaces through orthogonal constraints~\cite{dong2026deon}, and learns invariant representations through counterfactual distribution intervention~\cite{yuan2026cdi}. We study accuracy-constrained admission with asymmetric base and novel evidence. CLIP-guided discovery~\cite{yan2025clipdiscovery} additionally uses unlabeled novel data, whereas our arriving classes provide names and labeled support. FeNeC~\cite{ksiazek2026fenec} supplies a clustered-representative readout for controlled frozen-feature comparisons.

BiMC~\cite{chen2025bimc} and HyCal~\cite{lee2026hycal} calibrate multimodal or heterogeneous recognition metrics. Calibrated seen/unseen recognition~\cite{chao2016calibrated} studies the related operating trade-off, and Learn then Test~\cite{angelopoulos2021ltt} supplies risk-controlled selection. Our design couples vocabulary-invariant evidence alignment with finite-sample admission control. Query moments shape the recognition frontier; independent calibration selects a policy. The statistical constructions build on risk-controlled testing and bounded-mean betting~\cite{waudby2024betting}. We derive their consequences for class-macro retention, unequal calibration coverage and retained versus replacement error structures. An author-code-checked HyCal transfer supplies a matched heterogeneous-metric comparison.

\section{Reference alignment and certified admission}
\subsection{Vocabulary-invariant alignment of asymmetric evidence}
Base classes $\mathcal B$ have labeled, potentially imbalanced fitting data. Arriving classes $\mathcal N$ provide a name and $k$ labeled support images per class. Prediction selects from $\mathcal B\cup\mathcal N$ without a group oracle. A frozen encoder produces a normalized feature $v=f(x)$; normalized text anchors $t_c$ average eight fixed prompts. Learned base scores are $q_L(x,b)=v^\top w_b+b_b$, fitted by inverse-frequency-weighted ridge regression. Novel prototypes are normalized support means $p_n$, giving $q_P(x,n)=v^\top p_n$; semantic scores are $q_T(x,c)=v^\top t_c$.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering\includegraphics[width=.98\textwidth]{framework.pdf}
\caption{Reference-anchored asymmetric readout. Fixed base references set each branch's score frame; learned base columns remain stored as novel text anchors and support prototypes are appended. The separate novel offset controls admission. Orange marks base-fitted evidence; blue hatching marks pre-trained or cached evidence.}
\label{fig:framework}
\end{figure}

For each evidence source $e$, a fixed base reference bank $\mathcal R_e$ defines
\begin{align}
 \mu_e(x)&=|\mathcal R_e|^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal R_e}q_e(x,b),\\
 \sigma_e^2(x)&=|\mathcal R_e|^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal R_e}(q_e(x,b)-\mu_e(x))^2.
 \label{eq:reference}
\end{align}
We use base text anchors, learned columns and visual means as the text, learned and prototype references. Normalized evidence is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat q_e(x,c)=\frac{q_e(x,c)-\mu_e(x)}{\max(\sigma_e(x),10^{-6})}.
 \label{eq:anchored}
\end{equation}
The statistics adapt to each query while remaining independent of the active novel vocabulary. Fusion uses the evidence available to each row:
\begin{align}
 s(x,b)&=\widehat q_T(x,b)+a\widehat q_L(x,b),&&b\in\mathcal B,\\
 s(x,n)&=\widehat q_T(x,n)+u\widehat q_P(x,n)+d,&&n\in\mathcal N.
 \label{eq:base-novel}
\end{align}
The coefficients $a,u$ combine evidence; $d$ controls novel admission (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). Removing the text-centering shift shared by every candidate exposes the effective offset
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)=d+a\frac{\mu_L(x)}{\sigma_L(x)}-u\frac{\mu_P(x)}{\sigma_P(x)},
 \label{eq:effectiveadmission}
\end{equation}
using the floored scales in Eq.~\eqref{eq:anchored}. Centering adjusts the group margin for each query; scaling also changes within-group fusion.

\paragraph{Why the reference must be fixed}
Consider old text scores $(0,2)$ and a learned contribution $(0.25,-0.25)$. Candidate-standardized text scores $(-1,1)$ make the second old class win after fusion. Appending a text score of $100$ changes the old standardized scores to approximately $(-0.728,-0.685)$, reversing their fused order without changing either old class's parameters. Fixed reference banks prevent this vocabulary-induced change.

\begin{proposition}[Vocabulary-growth invariance]
With fixed input, encoder, existing evidence, reference banks, coefficients and tie-breaking, appending classes preserves every existing score and its within-vocabulary ordering.
\label{prop:invariance}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every term in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:reference}--\eqref{eq:base-novel} for an existing class remains fixed. Hence each pairwise score difference remains fixed.
\end{proof}
This property concerns stored discrimination; a newly appended class can still win the expanded prediction. Raw and fixed-temperature readouts also preserve scores, providing controls that isolate query alignment.

\begin{proposition}[Ridge identity and adaptive admission margin]
Let the weighted ridge fit have one-hot targets, an unregularized intercept and a unique solution. Its learned reference mean satisfies $\mu_L(x)=1/B$, where $B=|\mathcal B|$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)=d+\frac{a}{B\sigma_L(x)}-u\frac{\mu_P(x)}{\sigma_P(x)}.
 \label{eq:ridgeoffset}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $X$ include the constant feature, $Y$ contain one-hot targets, $\Omega$ contain sample weights, and $L$ regularize slopes. For the intercept vector $e_0$, $Xe_0=\mathbf1$, $Le_0=0$ and $Y\mathbf1=\mathbf1$. The normal equations give
$(X^\top\Omega X+L)W\mathbf1=(X^\top\Omega X+L)e_0$.
Uniqueness implies $W\mathbf1=e_0$; summing the $B$ learned scores gives one. Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:effectiveadmission} completes the result.
\end{proof}
Thus the learned part of the adaptive offset varies through score dispersion, while the prototype reference contributes both its query mean and dispersion. The combined margin determines admission. Each new class needs $O(D)$ storage and $O(kD)$ prototype construction after encoding; scoring $K$ classes costs $O(KD)$.

\subsection{The retention envelope}
\label{sec:calibration}
A base-only pseudo-expansion episode splits base classes into pseudo-base and pseudo-novel groups. A classifier fitted on pseudo-base classes is combined with names and reserved support for pseudo-novel classes. Held-out base queries select $a,u,d$ by harmonic macro accuracy,
\begin{equation}
 H=2A_BA_N/(A_B+A_N),
 \label{eq:harmonic}
\end{equation}
with zero assigned when both accuracies vanish. The coefficients then remain fixed across actual arrivals. Actual novel query labels and test labels are excluded from this selection; grids and split seeds are in Supplementary Section K.

Let $A_{B,\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{closed}}$ denote the retained readout's closed-base macro accuracy. At retention fraction $0<r<1$, every readout receives the same absolute base target: a 90\% reference gives an 85.5\% target at 95\% retention. Empirical expansion capacity is
\begin{equation}
 N_r=\max_d A_N(d)\quad\text{subject to}\quad
 A_B(d)\geq rA_{B,\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{closed}}.
 \label{eq:retentioncriterion}
\end{equation}
For a fixed readout, let $h_B,h_N$ be the best labels within each group and $m(x)$ their score difference before adding $d$. Increasing $d$ switches each query from $h_B$ to $h_N$ once, at $m(x)$, subject to the fixed tie rule. Thus $A_B(d)$ is nonincreasing and $A_N(d)$ is nondecreasing. The largest feasible event maximizes Eq.~\eqref{eq:retentioncriterion} for retained and replacement readouts. Test-label sweeps describe capacity; independent calibration selects deployable thresholds.

\begin{corollary}[Preserved base error structure]
Let $R$ indicate correctness of the fixed retained readout restricted to base classes and $C_d$ correctness after novel admission. Then $C_d\leq R$ on base queries. Consequently recovery $G=\Pr(R=0,C_d=1)=0$, and all destruction $D=\Pr(R=1,C_d=0)$ goes to novel labels:
\begin{equation}
 A_B(d)=A_{\mathrm{ref}}-D.
 \label{eq:retainederrors}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
The reference in this statement uses the same frozen fusion coefficients. Independently retuned readouts are compared experimentally against the common original reference, relative to which recovery is allowed.

\subsection{Finite-sample retention certification}
\label{sec:riskcontrol}
For every readout, let $R$ denote correctness of the same original closed-base reference and $C_d$ correctness after expansion. With destruction $D$ and recovery $G$, $A_B=A_{\mathrm{ref}}-D+G$. Define the protection loss
\begin{equation}
 L_d=R(1-C_d)+(1-r)(1-R),\qquad \alpha=1-r.
 \label{eq:protectionloss}
\end{equation}
Its risk is $D+\alpha(1-A_{\mathrm{ref}})$. Thus $\mathbb E L_d\leq\alpha$ is sufficient for retention and, when $G=0$, equivalent to $A_B\geq rA_{\mathrm{ref}}$. This is the direct connection between preserved base rankings and the accuracy-control objective.

We specialize bounded-loss concentration within the Learn then Test framework~\cite{angelopoulos2021ltt} to class-macro retention. The resulting weighted certificate exposes the role of tail-class coverage.

\begin{theorem}[Macro protection and tail coverage]
For independent class-stratified calibration losses in $[0,1]$, set $w_i=1/(Bn_{c(i)})$, $b=\max_iw_i$, and $q=\sum_iw_iL_i$. A valid p-value for the null $\sum_iw_i\mathbb E L_i\geq\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}
 p(q)=\begin{cases}\exp[-\operatorname{kl}(q,\alpha)/b],&q<\alpha,\\1,&q\geq\alpha.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:weightedprotection}
\end{equation}
At level $\delta/T$, passing this bound requires
\begin{equation}
 n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/b=B\min_c n_c\geq\frac{\log(\delta/T)}{\log r}.
 \label{eq:coveragefloor}
\end{equation}
\label{thm:protection}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $\mu=\sum_iw_i\mathbb E L_i$ and $x<\mu$, the bounded-loss Chernoff argument yields $\Pr(q\leq x)\leq\exp[-\operatorname{kl}(x,\mu)/b]$ (Supplementary Section J). Monotonicity in $\mu\geq\alpha$ gives the p-value. Its minimum $p(0)=r^{1/b}$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:coveragefloor}.
\end{proof}
The least-covered class controls this certificate's effective count. The condition is necessary for this bound; observed losses determine whether a covered candidate passes.

We adapt bounded-mean betting~\cite{waudby2024betting} to paired correctness with class-macro weights, so that the direct test credits recovered reference errors.

\begin{theorem}[Recovery-aware direct accuracy test]
For independent correctness pairs with the same macro weights, let $Z_i=C_{d,i}-rR_i$. For any fixed $0\leq\lambda<1/r$,
\begin{equation}
 E_\lambda=\prod_i[1+\lambda(w_i/b)Z_i]
 \label{eq:directbet}
\end{equation}
is nonnegative and has expectation at most one under $\sum_iw_i\mathbb E Z_i\leq0$. A fixed convex mixture $E$ therefore gives a valid fixed-sample p-value $\min(1,1/E)$ for the unsafe accuracy null.
\label{thm:direct}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each factor is positive because $Z_i\geq-r$ and $w_i/b\leq1$. Independence and $1+x\leq e^x$ give
$\mathbb E E_\lambda\leq\exp[(\lambda/b)\sum_iw_i\mathbb E Z_i]\leq1$.
Linearity and Markov's inequality establish the mixture result.
\end{proof}
We use twelve fixed mixture components, specified in Supplementary Section N. Sample sizes and mixture weights are fixed independently of calibration outcomes.

Both tests operate on thresholds fixed by fitting/tuning data, ordered from conservative to permissive. Fixed-sequence testing~\cite{angelopoulos2021ltt} stops at the first failure at level $\delta/T$ and returns the last accepted threshold. If none passes, the policy uses the original reference with zero novel admission. A union bound controls unsafe acceptance across $T=5$ prespecified arrivals with probability at most $\delta$. The statement holds per fixed method/order/partition/policy under independent target class-conditional sampling; calibration data are excluded from fitting and tuning. The reference fallback and finite admission are reported separately.

\paragraph{Error structure and paired-score variability}
For a class-macro base query, let $A_C=\mathbb E C$ and $A_R=\mathbb E R$. Direct expansion of $Z=C-rR$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Var}(Z)={}&A_R(1-r)^2+(A_R-A_C)(2r-1)\\
 &+2rG-(A_C-rA_R)^2.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:pairedvariance}
\end{equation}
At fixed $A_R,A_C$, recovery accompanied by additional destruction increases variance by $2rG$ relative to a feasible zero-recovery coupling. Retention removes that component while also making protection exact. For stratified calibration, the variance of the weighted mean is $\sum_c\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid c)/(B^2n_c)$. The analogous variance comparison requires matching both accuracies within every class and sharing calibration counts. Actual certification also depends on accuracy slack, class coverage and the threshold family. Supplementary Section P derives the identity and its scope.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Protocol and comparisons}
\label{sec:tenpartitions}
We evaluate CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009cifar}, Oxford-IIIT Pets~\cite{parkhi2012cats} and ImageNet-R~\cite{hendrycks2021manyfaces}, using frozen OpenAI CLIP ViT-L/14 and ViT-B/32 features, five-shot support, ten frequency-stratified class partitions and two five-session arrival orders. Base/novel counts are 40/40, 15/15 and 100/100; each session adds eight, three or 20 classes. Base pools follow long-tailed sampling. ImageNet-R uses task-specific splits after removing byte-identical duplicates. Novelty is relative to task fitting, with CLIP's pre-training exposure retained. Detailed pools, preprocessing and seeds are in Supplementary Sections C, I and K.

Every comparator shares its encoder, partition and support. We compare fixed-gate classifier removal, independently recalibrated removal, dense anchored text--prototype fusion, summed/class-mean Tip-Adapter caches, and raw/fixed-temperature retained fusion. The latter uses a single base-validation scale per branch. HyCal uses the author's heterogeneous cosine/Mahalanobis metrics, count weighting and Gaussian-CDF calibration, transferred to the same eight-prompt anchors and frozen encoders. Its covariance regularization and novel offset are selected by base-only pseudo-expansion. Supplementary Section M documents the pinned implementation, numerical fixtures and transfer settings. All mixtures use base-only selection; FeNeC and earlier three-partition auxiliary studies are retained in Supplementary Sections A--H.

Orders are averaged within a partition. Means and standard deviations describe ten class assignments on fixed images. Paired differences use identical cells; constrained comparisons use jointly feasible sets. Conditional paired 95\% bootstrap intervals resample partition means 20,000 times. Average incremental accuracy is the mean macro accuracy over five arrivals. These intervals characterize class-assignment variation, conditional on the fixed data and encoders.

\subsection{Query-adaptive alignment expands retention frontiers}
Independent fusion tuning isolates query normalization under equal search budgets, with encoder, ridge fit, support and data roles fixed. At the common 95\% base target, query normalization exceeds uncentered fusion by 3.60 [2.55,4.74] points on ImageNet-R L/14 and 5.29 [3.74,7.25] on B/32. Gains over fixed global normalization are 2.84 [2.42,3.28] and 4.65 [3.57,5.71] points. Each comparison includes ten partitions after averaging orders. Supplementary Section Q gives the factorial and search specification; Section M separately isolates centering and scaling with fusion held fixed.

The adaptive offset acts through the combined group margin in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ridgeoffset}. On the original frozen models, adding its centering term raises base/novel ranking AUROC from .924 to .977 on ImageNet-R L/14 and from .856 to .904 on B/32. The term alone has novel-positive AUROC below .5 in all six conditions: its role is to align evidence within the decision margin. Section P supplies the conditional distributions and offset-reselected controls.

Independent tuning also gives a retained-minus-dense capacity gap of 9.69 [8.08,11.21] points on ImageNet-R B/32 and 0.25 [$-0.46$,0.92] on L/14. The next analyses examine how these recognition structures support repeated and certified admission.

\subsection{From recognition capacity to certified policies}
Figure~\ref{fig:fairness} holds calibration images, candidate thresholds and error allocation fixed while changing the statistical criterion. On ImageNet-R L/14, crediting recovered errors raises dense-prototype admission from 9/10 to 10/10 and novel accuracy from 48.58\% to 76.76\%, including fallback. Retained readouts attain 81.61\%, a paired gain of 4.85 [2.78,6.93] points with all ten partitions admitted by both methods.

Let $F_m(t)$ be readout $m$'s empirical novel capacity at base requirement $t$, $N_m$ its selected-policy novel accuracy, and $H_m(t)=F_m(t)-N_m$ its remaining capacity. For retained $R$ and dense $P$ readouts,
\begin{equation}
 N_R-N_P=\underbrace{F_R(t)-F_P(t)}_{\text{capacity difference}}+\underbrace{H_P(t)-H_R(t)}_{\text{remaining-capacity difference}}.
 \label{eq:policydecomposition}
\end{equation}
On ImageNet-R L/14, capacities 83.93\% and 83.64\% differ by 0.29 points, while remaining capacities are 2.32 and 6.88 points. The latter difference accounts for 4.56 points of the 4.85-point policy gap. Comparable capacity can therefore coexist with different certified operating points. The remaining capacity combines threshold discretization, sampling and selection effects; this decomposition does not assign all of it to test variance.

Table~\ref{tab:policycapacity} extends the analysis across datasets and encoders. The 35.40-point CIFAR L/14 policy gap includes reference fallback, alongside a 3.68-point capacity gap. Pets L/14 has a $-2.12$-point retained-minus-dense capacity gap and a positive policy gap. ImageNet-R B/32 additionally supplies a 10.40-point capacity advantage. Figure~\ref{fig:operatingMain} overlays selected policies on exact empirical trajectories, displaying both capacity and how it is used.

\subsection{Target calibration transfers frozen source readouts}
\label{sec:shift}
The controlled CIFAR stress test uses clean fitting/support and noisy or blurred queries. Clean calibration meets 5/10 and 1/10 L/14 final targets; matching calibration restores 10/10 with finite admission for both encoders (Section O).

The separate transfer study freezes the original source-selected ImageNet-R readouts and evaluates matching ImageNet-Sketch classes~\cite{wang2019robustglobal}. Identity-disjoint target calibration and test pools are fixed before scoring (Supplementary Section R). Source and Sketch calibration use equal per-class counts; model parameters and source support remain fixed. With direct Sketch calibration, retained readouts reach 86.82\% and 58.96\% novel accuracy with L/14 and B/32, respectively, with finite admission and target attainment in all ten partitions. Source calibration also meets these targets, at 82.99\% and 54.87\% novel accuracy. At the common 95\% minimum target, L/14 retained and dense capacities both round to 88.84\%; B/32 retention supplies a 5.78 [4.03,7.55]-point capacity gain on nine jointly feasible partitions. Section R reports the identity exclusions, absolute base accuracies, both calibration criteria and exact frontiers.

\section{Conclusion}
Reference-anchored evidence alignment supports class expansion with finite-sample retention control. Fixed base references preserve existing discrimination as the vocabulary grows, while query normalization induces an adaptive admission margin. Preserved base rankings make protection coincide with the accuracy requirement; a recovery-aware test extends certification to replacement readouts. Controlled comparisons establish the normalization mechanism, and capacity-to-policy analysis shows how comparable recognition frontiers can yield different certified admission. Target calibration transfers frozen source readouts to natural rendition shift without retraining. The framework connects stable evidence, explicit accuracy requirements and useful admission across successive class arrivals.
