\begin{abstract}
Class expansion combines a trained base classifier with names and few-shot prototypes for arriving classes. We propose reference-anchored asymmetric class expansion, which aligns heterogeneous evidence against fixed base references and preserves existing rankings as the vocabulary grows. Query normalization induces a sample-adaptive admission margin without an auxiliary router; independent calibration selects its global offset under a base-accuracy constraint. Across five ImageNet-R arrivals, the method improves average incremental accuracy over a matched HyCal transfer by 10.93 and 13.74 percentage points with CLIP L/14 and B/32. Independently retuned query normalization adds 3.60 and 5.29 points of novel capacity at 95\% retention. Frozen source models reach 86.82\% and 58.96\% novel accuracy on ImageNet-Sketch after target calibration, meeting empirical retention targets in all ten partitions per encoder.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A recognition system accumulates unequal evidence as its category vocabulary grows. A trained base classifier encodes supervised discrimination, while arriving classes provide category names and a few labeled images. Reusing both sources requires a joint decision across base and novel classes without a task or group oracle. The central challenge is to align their evidence while preserving the discrimination already fitted to the base task.

Pretrained representations make lightweight expansion practical. SimpleCIL~\cite{zhou2024simplecil} and RanPAC~\cite{mcdonnell2023ranpac} develop prototype and analytic readouts; BiMC~\cite{chen2025bimc} and HyCal~\cite{lee2026hycal} calibrate multimodal or heterogeneous recognition metrics. Our setting retains the learned base classifier alongside semantic anchors and novel visual prototypes. These branches have different score origins and scales. Standardizing against the active candidate vocabulary introduces a further dependence: appending a class can change branch scales and reverse an existing fused ranking, even when all existing parameters remain fixed.

We propose \emph{reference-anchored asymmetric class expansion}. Fixed base reference banks give each branch a stable coordinate system throughout vocabulary growth. Their statistics adapt to the query, aligning learned, semantic and prototype evidence before fusion. This construction preserves existing scores while producing a query-dependent base--novel admission margin. A ridge identity, $\mu_L(x)=1/|\mathcal B|$, makes the learned contribution explicit: centered class-specific responses compete with prototype evidence measured relative to base visual references. The resulting joint decision requires no auxiliary routing network.

The method separates query-adaptive alignment from the system's admission requirement. Once the readout is fixed, a single global offset sets the base--novel operating point. Independent calibration chooses this offset to meet a specified base-accuracy requirement over successive arrivals. The same separation supports transfer: source-fitted classifiers, fusion parameters and novel prototypes remain frozen while target calibration adjusts admission.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item An asymmetric readout that retains learned base discrimination and incorporates few-shot novel evidence in a vocabulary-invariant reference frame.
\item An explicit query-adaptive admission mechanism, supported by the ridge mean identity, controlled centering/scaling comparisons and independently retuned fusion experiments.
\item Retention-constrained expansion and frozen-model transfer, with finite-sample admission control and matched comparisons across three source datasets, two CLIP encoders and a natural rendering shift.
\end{itemize}

On ImageNet-R, the readout exceeds a matched HyCal transfer by 10.93 and 13.74 points in average incremental accuracy with L/14 and B/32. Query centering raises L/14 base--novel ranking AUROC from .924 to .977; independent fusion retuning confirms capacity gains of 3.60 and 5.29 points over uncentered fusion. On ImageNet-Sketch, target calibration gives 86.82\% and 58.96\% novel accuracy with empirical retention attainment in every evaluated partition. These results connect the alignment mechanism to sustained recognition and usable class expansion.

\section{Related work}
Long-tailed recognition addresses unequal class supervision through balanced fitting and losses~\cite{liu2019large,cao2019ldam,cui2019classbalanced}. CLIP supplies semantic image--text evidence~\cite{radford2021clip}, while prompt adaptation~\cite{zhou2022coop,shi2024lift}, prototypes~\cite{snell2017prototypical,wang2019simpleshot} and semantic caches~\cite{zhang2022tipadapter} adapt pre-trained representations to labeled data. Our setting combines these evidence types according to their availability at class arrival.

Class-incremental and few-shot incremental learning study retention across successive tasks~\cite{rebuffi2017icarl,tao2020fewshot}. SimpleCIL~\cite{zhou2024simplecil} and RanPAC~\cite{mcdonnell2023ranpac} show the value of pre-trained prototypes and analytic readouts. KBS approaches develop multi-feature similarity supplementation~\cite{xu2023mfs3}, sharpness-aware prototype calibration~\cite{chen2024sagg}, adaptive exploration~\cite{han2025adaptive}, representation-enhanced analytic learning~\cite{he2026real}, and multimodal prompt fusion~\cite{fang2026bpcclip}.

Recent work aligns task-specific expert inference in MoTE~\cite{li2025mote}, separates expanding feature spaces through orthogonal constraints~\cite{dong2026deon}, and learns invariant representations through counterfactual distribution intervention~\cite{yuan2026cdi}. We study accuracy-constrained admission with asymmetric base and novel evidence. CLIP-guided discovery~\cite{yan2025clipdiscovery} additionally uses unlabeled novel data, whereas our arriving classes provide names and labeled support. FeNeC~\cite{ksiazek2026fenec} supplies a clustered-representative readout for controlled frozen-feature comparisons.

BiMC~\cite{chen2025bimc} and HyCal~\cite{lee2026hycal} calibrate multimodal or heterogeneous recognition metrics. We align asymmetric evidence against fixed base references, so query adaptation and vocabulary growth have distinct roles. Calibrated seen/unseen recognition~\cite{chao2016calibrated} supplies a related operating trade-off. For retention-constrained deployment, we specialize risk-controlled selection~\cite{angelopoulos2021ltt} and bounded-mean betting~\cite{waudby2024betting} to class-macro base accuracy. A matched HyCal transfer evaluates recognition under the same encoder, support and data access.

\section{Reference-anchored asymmetric class expansion}
\subsection{Vocabulary-invariant alignment of asymmetric evidence}
Base classes $\mathcal B$ have labeled, potentially imbalanced fitting data. Arriving classes $\mathcal N$ provide a name and $k$ labeled support images per class. Prediction selects from $\mathcal B\cup\mathcal N$ without a group oracle. A frozen encoder produces a normalized feature $v=f(x)$; normalized text anchors $t_c$ average eight fixed prompts. Learned base scores are $q_L(x,b)=v^\top w_b+b_b$, fitted by inverse-frequency-weighted ridge regression. Novel prototypes are normalized support means $p_n$, giving $q_P(x,n)=v^\top p_n$; semantic scores are $q_T(x,c)=v^\top t_c$.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering\includegraphics[width=.98\textwidth]{framework.pdf}
\caption{Reference-anchored asymmetric readout. Fixed base references set each branch's score frame; learned base columns remain stored as novel text anchors and support prototypes are appended. The separate novel offset controls admission. Orange marks base-fitted evidence; blue hatching marks pre-trained or cached evidence.}
\label{fig:framework}
\end{figure}

For each evidence source $e$, a fixed base reference bank $\mathcal R_e$ defines
\begin{align}
 \mu_e(x)&=|\mathcal R_e|^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal R_e}q_e(x,b),\\
 \sigma_e^2(x)&=|\mathcal R_e|^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal R_e}(q_e(x,b)-\mu_e(x))^2.
 \label{eq:reference}
\end{align}
We use base text anchors, learned columns and visual means as the text, learned and prototype references. Normalized evidence is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat q_e(x,c)=\frac{q_e(x,c)-\mu_e(x)}{\max(\sigma_e(x),10^{-6})}.
 \label{eq:anchored}
\end{equation}
The statistics adapt to each query while remaining independent of the active novel vocabulary. Fusion uses the evidence available to each row:
\begin{align}
 s(x,b)&=\widehat q_T(x,b)+a\widehat q_L(x,b),&&b\in\mathcal B,\\
 s(x,n)&=\widehat q_T(x,n)+u\widehat q_P(x,n)+d,&&n\in\mathcal N.
 \label{eq:base-novel}
\end{align}
The coefficients $a,u$ combine evidence; $d$ controls novel admission (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). Removing the text-centering shift shared by every candidate exposes the effective offset
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)=d+a\frac{\mu_L(x)}{\sigma_L(x)}-u\frac{\mu_P(x)}{\sigma_P(x)},
 \label{eq:effectiveadmission}
\end{equation}
using the floored scales in Eq.~\eqref{eq:anchored}. Centering adjusts the group margin for each query; scaling also changes within-group fusion.

\paragraph{Why the reference must be fixed}
Consider old text scores $(0,2)$ and a learned contribution $(0.25,-0.25)$. Candidate-standardized text scores $(-1,1)$ make the second old class win after fusion. Appending a text score of $100$ changes the old standardized scores to approximately $(-0.728,-0.685)$, reversing their fused order without changing either old class's parameters. Fixed reference banks prevent this vocabulary-induced change.

\begin{proposition}[Vocabulary-growth invariance]
With fixed input, encoder, existing evidence, reference banks, coefficients and tie-breaking, appending classes preserves every existing score and its within-vocabulary ordering.
\label{prop:invariance}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every term in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:reference}--\eqref{eq:base-novel} for an existing class remains fixed. Hence each pairwise score difference remains fixed.
\end{proof}
This property concerns stored discrimination; a newly appended class can still win the expanded prediction. Raw and fixed-temperature readouts also preserve scores, providing controls that isolate query alignment.

\subsection{Query-adaptive base--novel admission}
\begin{proposition}[Ridge identity and adaptive admission margin]
Let the weighted ridge fit have one-hot targets, an unregularized intercept and a unique solution. Its learned reference mean satisfies $\mu_L(x)=1/B$, where $B=|\mathcal B|$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)=d+\frac{a}{B\sigma_L(x)}-u\frac{\mu_P(x)}{\sigma_P(x)}.
 \label{eq:ridgeoffset}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $X$ include the constant feature, $Y$ contain one-hot targets, $\Omega$ contain sample weights, and $L$ regularize slopes. For the intercept vector $e_0$, $Xe_0=\mathbf1$, $Le_0=0$ and $Y\mathbf1=\mathbf1$. The normal equations give
$(X^\top\Omega X+L)W\mathbf1=(X^\top\Omega X+L)e_0$.
Uniqueness implies $W\mathbf1=e_0$; summing the $B$ learned scores gives one. Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:effectiveadmission} completes the result.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{How alignment determines admission}
The learned score contributes its deviation from the common ridge mean, $(q_L(x,b)-1/B)/\sigma_L(x)$. The novel prototype contributes its similarity relative to base visual references, $(q_P(x,n)-\mu_P(x))/\sigma_P(x)$. With the common text-centering term removed, their competition is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
M(x)={}&\max_{n\in\mathcal N}\left[\frac{q_T(x,n)}{\sigma_T(x)}+u\frac{q_P(x,n)-\mu_P(x)}{\sigma_P(x)}\right]\\
&-\max_{b\in\mathcal B}\left[\frac{q_T(x,b)}{\sigma_T(x)}+a\frac{q_L(x,b)-1/B}{\sigma_L(x)}\right].
\end{split}
\label{eq:jointmargin}
\end{equation}
The system selects the novel group when $M(x)+d>0$ and the base group when it is negative, with the fixed class-ID rule resolving ties. Query scales make branch responses comparable; query centers adjust their relative group evidence. The scalar $d$ then shifts this adaptive decision to the desired retention level. The mechanism uses the existing readout responses and requires no auxiliary router or task label. Its empirical signature is improved ranking of base versus novel queries together with greater correctly classified novel admission.

Each new class needs $O(D)$ storage and $O(kD)$ prototype construction after encoding; scoring $K$ classes costs $O(KD)$. Fixed references preserve the meaning of $M(x)$ as additional classes enter the maximization.

\subsection{The retention envelope}
\label{sec:calibration}
A base-only pseudo-expansion episode splits base classes into pseudo-base and pseudo-novel groups. A classifier fitted on pseudo-base classes is combined with names and reserved support for pseudo-novel classes. Held-out base queries select $a,u,d$ by harmonic macro accuracy,
\begin{equation}
 H=2A_BA_N/(A_B+A_N),
 \label{eq:harmonic}
\end{equation}
with zero assigned when both accuracies vanish. The coefficients then remain fixed across actual arrivals. Actual novel query labels and test labels are excluded from this selection; grids and split seeds are in Supplementary Section K.

Let $A_{B,\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{closed}}$ denote the retained readout's closed-base macro accuracy. At retention fraction $0<r<1$, every readout receives the same absolute base target: a 90\% reference gives an 85.5\% target at 95\% retention. Empirical expansion capacity is
\begin{equation}
 N_r=\max_d A_N(d)\quad\text{subject to}\quad
 A_B(d)\geq rA_{B,\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{closed}}.
 \label{eq:retentioncriterion}
\end{equation}
For a fixed readout, let $h_B,h_N$ be the best labels within each group and $m(x)$ their score difference before adding $d$. Increasing $d$ switches each query from $h_B$ to $h_N$ once, at $m(x)$, subject to the fixed tie rule. Thus $A_B(d)$ is nonincreasing and $A_N(d)$ is nondecreasing. The largest feasible event maximizes Eq.~\eqref{eq:retentioncriterion} for retained and replacement readouts. Test-label sweeps describe capacity; independent calibration selects deployable thresholds.

\begin{corollary}[Preserved base error structure]
Let $R$ indicate correctness of the fixed retained readout restricted to base classes and $C_d$ correctness after novel admission. Then $C_d\leq R$ on base queries. Consequently recovery $G=\Pr(R=0,C_d=1)=0$, and all destruction $D=\Pr(R=1,C_d=0)$ goes to novel labels:
\begin{equation}
 A_B(d)=A_{\mathrm{ref}}-D.
 \label{eq:retainederrors}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
The reference in this statement uses the same frozen fusion coefficients. Independently retuned readouts are compared experimentally against the common original reference, relative to which recovery is allowed.

\subsection{Selecting an offset under a retention requirement}
\label{sec:riskcontrol}
Calibration converts the aligned readout into an admission policy. Let $R$ indicate correctness of the original closed-base reference and $C_d$ correctness after expansion. For destruction $D$ and recovery $G$, $A_B=A_{\mathrm{ref}}-D+G$. With $\alpha=1-r$, define
\begin{equation}
L_d=R(1-C_d)+(1-r)(1-R).
\label{eq:protectionloss}
\end{equation}
The condition $\mathbb E L_d\leq\alpha$ guarantees $A_B\geq rA_{\mathrm{ref}}$. For the original retained readout, $G=0$ makes these requirements equivalent. We specialize bounded-loss concentration within the Learn then Test framework~\cite{angelopoulos2021ltt} to unequal class-macro calibration weights.

\begin{theorem}[Macro protection and tail coverage]
For independent class-stratified losses in $[0,1]$, let $w_i=1/(Bn_{c(i)})$, $b=\max_i w_i$ and $q=\sum_iw_iL_i$. A valid p-value for $\sum_iw_i\mathbb E L_i\geq\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}
p(q)=\begin{cases}\exp[-\operatorname{kl}(q,\alpha)/b],&q<\alpha,\\1,&q\geq\alpha.\end{cases}
\label{eq:weightedprotection}
\end{equation}
Passing this bound at level $\delta/T$ requires
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{eff}}=B\min_c n_c\geq\frac{\log(\delta/T)}{\log r}.
\label{eq:coveragefloor}
\end{equation}
\label{thm:protection}
\end{theorem}
The weighted Chernoff proof is in Supplementary Section J; the necessary coverage condition follows from the bound's minimum $p(0)=r^{1/b}$. Actual admission also depends on observed losses.

We adapt bounded-mean betting~\cite{waudby2024betting} to test the accuracy requirement directly through $Z_i=C_{d,i}-rR_i$.
\begin{theorem}[Direct paired accuracy test]
For independent correctness pairs and any fixed $0\leq\lambda<1/r$,
\begin{equation}
E_\lambda=\prod_i[1+\lambda(w_i/b)Z_i]
\label{eq:directbet}
\end{equation}
is nonnegative with expectation at most one under $\sum_iw_i\mathbb E Z_i\leq0$. A fixed convex mixture $E$ gives a valid fixed-sample p-value $\min(1,1/E)$.
\label{thm:direct}
\end{theorem}
Supplementary Section N gives the proof and twelve fixed mixture components. This test uses the same accuracy requirement for every readout, including changes that recover reference errors.

Threshold candidates are fixed using tuning data and tested from conservative to permissive. Fixed-sequence testing stops at the first failure at level $\delta/T$, returning the last accepted offset; if none passes, the original reference supplies zero novel admission. A union bound controls unsafe acceptance over $T=5$ prespecified arrivals with probability at most $\delta$. This confidence statement is per fixed method/order/partition/policy under independent target class-conditional sampling, with calibration excluded from fitting and tuning. The same calibration images serve all methods. Section P gives the conditional error-structure and variance analysis.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Protocol and comparisons}
\label{sec:tenpartitions}
We evaluate CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009cifar}, Oxford-IIIT Pets~\cite{parkhi2012cats} and ImageNet-R~\cite{hendrycks2021manyfaces}, using frozen OpenAI CLIP ViT-L/14 and ViT-B/32 features, five-shot support, ten frequency-stratified class partitions and two five-session arrival orders. Base/novel counts are 40/40, 15/15 and 100/100; each session adds eight, three or 20 classes. Base pools follow long-tailed sampling. ImageNet-R uses task-specific splits after removing byte-identical duplicates. Novelty is relative to task fitting, with CLIP's pre-training exposure retained. Detailed pools, preprocessing and seeds are in Supplementary Sections C, I and K.

Every comparator shares its encoder, partition and support. We compare fixed-gate classifier removal, independently recalibrated removal, dense anchored text--prototype fusion, summed/class-mean Tip-Adapter caches, and raw/fixed-temperature retained fusion. The latter uses a single base-validation scale per branch. HyCal uses the author's heterogeneous cosine/Mahalanobis metrics, count weighting and Gaussian-CDF calibration, transferred to the same eight-prompt anchors and frozen encoders. Its covariance regularization and novel offset are selected by base-only pseudo-expansion. Supplementary Section M documents the pinned implementation, numerical fixtures and transfer settings. All mixtures use base-only selection; FeNeC and earlier three-partition auxiliary studies are retained in Supplementary Sections A--H.

Orders are averaged within a partition. Means and standard deviations describe ten class assignments on fixed images. Paired differences use identical cells; constrained comparisons use jointly feasible sets. Conditional paired 95\% bootstrap intervals resample partition means 20,000 times. Average incremental accuracy is the mean macro accuracy over five arrivals. These intervals characterize class-assignment variation, conditional on the fixed data and encoders.

Section N compares both certificates under identical access, including dense L/14 ImageNet-R's 48.58\% protection result and 76.76\% direct result.

Equation~\eqref{eq:coveragefloor} gives the necessary effective count 89.78 for the protection bound. Nested per-class caps 2, 5, 10, 20 and 40 show how coverage supports admission (Section L). Cap 40 uses 1,600 base labels on CIFAR, 600 on Pets and 3,636--3,778 on ImageNet-R, additional to five-shot novel support. All readouts and tuning parameters remain fixed across these label budgets.

\subsection{Frozen-model transfer through target calibration}
\label{sec:shift}
The transfer study evaluates the original ImageNet-R readouts on matching ImageNet-Sketch classes~\cite{wang2019robustglobal}. The encoder, base classifier, fusion coefficients, five-shot source prototypes and candidate offsets remain fixed. Source and target calibration receive equal per-class counts; target labels select the scalar admission offset. Identity-disjoint target pools are fixed before scoring (Supplementary Section R).

With direct target calibration, retained readouts attain 86.82\% novel accuracy at 90.76\% base accuracy with L/14 and 58.96\% at 78.87\% with B/32. All five sessions admit expansion and meet their empirical test-retention targets in every partition. Dense L/14 reaches 83.34\% novel accuracy with 10/10 final admissions; dense B/32 reaches 8.35\% including fallback, with 3/10 admissions and 27.84\% accuracy conditional on admission. These results demonstrate useful transfer of the source readout through target-domain admission calibration.

\section{Conclusion}
Reference-anchored asymmetric class expansion preserves learned base discrimination while incorporating semantic and few-shot novel evidence. Fixed base references eliminate vocabulary-induced score changes; query normalization aligns heterogeneous responses and creates an explicit sample-adaptive admission margin. Controlled experiments connect this mechanism to stronger base--novel decisions and sustained incremental recognition. A separately calibrated scalar offset enforces a base-accuracy requirement and transfers frozen source models to a natural rendering shift. The method provides a coherent path from asymmetric evidence to stable, useful class expansion.
